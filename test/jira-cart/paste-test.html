The Paste Test

Nine fields per issue have to land somewhere a person will read them. A table wins in Confluence and Outlook and loses everywhere people actually chat — Slack has no tables at all, and a pasted list can be reshuffled by hand where a table cannot. So: five shapes, shown in the three places the clipboard really goes. Switch shapes and fields until one of them is obviously right.

What survives a chat paste

Links, bold and bullet lists survive everywhere. They are the common denominator across all seven targets above, which is what makes a list the safe shape.

A colour is only safe if it brings its own background. Flipping the client theme to dark found this: the status lozenge sets both background and text, so it survives either ground as a self-contained sticker — the fix-version chip set only text, and its fixed grey went unreadable.

Then Outlook killed the elegant fix. Pasted with keep source formatting on 2026-08-20, Outlook stripped every opacity and every inline border — it renders HTML through Word's engine. So the muting is one fixed grey instead, and the fix versions are joined by a comma rather than divided by boxes that turned out not to exist.

And Teams does its own theming. The same grey span came out dark in the light skin and light in the dark skin — Teams re-maps text colour to whatever skin it is in. So the grey chosen for Outlook costs nothing there, and the light-versus-dark problem simply does not exist in Teams.

That last row is the counterintuitive one. Making the pill bolder so it would survive is exactly the wrong move: Teams keeps a pale ground and discards a saturated one, then re-maps the white text to its own skin, so a bold pill loses its ground and its colour and comes back as bold black. The fix for an invisible pill is a slightly stronger tint, not a solid fill.

Two trailing spaces are markdown's hard line break. The two-line shape needs them, they are invisible, and some editors strip them on save. They are marked in the plain-text panel so you can see what you are relying on.

Reshuffling it afterwards

The paste is not the end. People reorder the list in the editor they pasted it into — to put the urgent thing first, or to group what they are talking about. How easily a shape can be reshuffled by hand is a real criterion, and it is the second argument against the table.

It also rules out anything positional. Printing the epic's name only on its first appearance would kill the repetition neatly — and would then be wrong the moment somebody moved a line, with nothing on the page to say so. A field either says the same thing on every row or it cannot be trusted after a reshuffle.

Deliberately absent — do not read as an omission

  • The two-step button. No fetch, no 📋 Table → Copy table 12 ladder, no ⚠️. That behaviour is already settled; this page is only about the shape of what comes out.
  • The Cart itself. No drawer, no live list, no collection chips. The shapes are shown in their destinations, not in the panel they are copied from.
  • Teams' and Slack's real renderers. The chat panel is a width and a typeface, not a simulation. It is honest about the 430px constraint and about nothing else — which is exactly why the Copy this shape for real button is there.
  • A markdown renderer. Nothing on this page turns markdown into HTML. The two rich panels show what the text/html flavour emits; the mono panel shows the text/plain bytes. They are two independent outputs, not one converted twice.
  • Field order. The order fields appear in the meta line is a proposal and nothing more. So is which fields default to on.
  • Reordering the collection. Not here, and not proposed. The reshuffling this page cares about happens after the paste, in Teams or Outlook or a text file, by hand. The Cart still emits the collection in insertion order and still has no reorder control.
